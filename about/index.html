About

A guide to making a Hanover rental cheaper and warmer to live in, written for students who have never paid a heating bill.

Why this site exists

Most home energy advice is written for people who own their home. It assumes you can replace a window, insulate an attic, or install a new heating system. A renter can do almost none of that, and a page that opens with those options is a page that stops being useful in its first paragraph.

Every improvement here states two things a homeowner guide never has to: whether you need your landlord's permission, and whether it comes off again when you move out. Those two answers are what decide whether advice is worth reading when the apartment is not yours.

Sustainable Hanover already publishes weatherization guidance for homeowners in town. This site adapts that work for renters and adds the part a static page cannot do: you tell it your heat type and who pays for it, and it narrows what it shows you.

How the content is sourced

Every number on this site traces to a named source, and each page lists its own at the bottom with the date it was last checked. Where no source exists, the number does not go on the page. Rates, rebates and program terms go stale faster than anything else here, so those pages carry the shortest review interval.

Content about tenant rights and about safety is reviewed by a person before it publishes. Neither is written from a search result.

The whole project is public, including the planning documents, the source list, and a record of how it was built. View the source code.

What this site will not do

  • Name a specific landlord or a specific property.
  • Recommend a brand, or link to a product for commission.
  • Collect anything about you. Your situation and your reduce-motion setting are stored in your own browser and are never sent anywhere.
  • Advise you on a dispute you are actually having. That needs a person, and Rights and programs says who.

The disclaimers

Legal: This site provides general information, not legal advice. Leases and situations differ.

Savings: Estimates are typical ranges, not guarantees. What you actually save depends on your building, your heating system, and the weather.

Permission: Check your lease before making any change. When in doubt, ask your landlord in writing.
