Cut your hot water use

Shower length, laundry temperature, and a dripping tap all affect the bill.

No permission needed Comes off at move-out
Free Under 30 minutes Medium impact

Steps

  1. Time one shower, then try shaving a few minutes off the next few.
  2. Switch your laundry machine's temperature setting to cold or warm instead of hot for regular loads.
  3. Check every tap in the unit for a drip, including ones you rarely use.
  4. Report any dripping tap to your landlord in writing. Fixing it is their job, not yours.

Why this works

Heating water takes real energy, so any hot water you do not use is energy you do not pay for. A shorter shower and a cooler wash cycle both cut how much water your unit's water heater has to warm up. A dripping tap wastes water continuously, all day and all night, and if it is the hot tap it is quietly wasting the energy that heated that water too.

Savings

Estimates are typical ranges, not guarantees. What you actually save depends on your building, your heating system, and the weather.

A single faucet dripping about once a second can waste more than 3,000 gallons of water a year, and the U.S. EPA estimates that fixing common household leaks saves about 10% on the water bill.
