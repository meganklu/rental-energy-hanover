Swap in a low-flow showerhead

A showerhead unscrews by hand. Less hot water through it is less oil or electricity burned heating it.

Ask your landlord first Comes off at move-out
Under $25 Under 30 minutes Medium impact

What you need

  • A showerhead carrying the WaterSense label
  • A roll of plumber's thread tape
  • A cloth, and an adjustable wrench only if the old one will not turn by hand
  • A bag to keep the original head in until you move out

Where to get it

Steps

  1. Ask your landlord first. It is their fixture, and a one-line email saying you would like to fit a water-saving head and will put the original back is usually the whole conversation.
  2. Turn the old head anticlockwise to unscrew it. Most come off by hand. If it will not budge, wrap the cloth around the nut so the wrench does not mark the finish.
  3. Wipe the threads on the shower arm clean.
  4. Wrap thread tape clockwise around the threads, three or four turns.
  5. Screw the new head on by hand until it is snug. Hand-tight is enough, and overtightening splits the plastic.
  6. Run it and check around the joint for drips. One more turn usually settles it.
  7. Put the original head in the bag and keep it somewhere you will find it in a year.

Why this works

A shower is not really a water bill for most students. It is a heating bill. Water arrives cold and something has to raise it to shower temperature, and that something runs on oil, propane, gas or electricity depending on what is in the basement.

A standard showerhead moves 2.5 gallons a minute. One carrying the EPA's WaterSense label moves no more than 2.0, and has to prove in independent testing that the spray still covers you properly at that rate. Every gallon that does not go through the head is a gallon nobody had to heat.

It is the same saving as taking a shorter shower, except it happens whether or not anyone remembers to.

Savings

Estimates are typical ranges, not guarantees. What you actually save depends on your building, your heating system, and the weather.

The EPA puts the saving from a WaterSense labeled showerhead at 2,700 gallons of water and more than 330 kilowatt hours of electricity a year for the average family. A student household is not an average family, so read that as the shape of the saving rather than as your number.

Showering is close to a fifth of the water used indoors in an average home, so of the places to spend twenty minutes, this is one of the better ones.

If hot water is included in your rent, this saves your landlord money and not you. That makes it a good thing to raise with them rather than to pay for yourself.

What to do next

  • Cut your hot water use covers the free half: shower length, laundry temperature, and the dripping tap that is costing you all winter.
