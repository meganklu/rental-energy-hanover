Seal your door with a sweep

Weatherstripping and a door sweep close the gap most exterior doors leak through.

Ask your landlord first Mostly comes off at move-out
Under $25 1 to 2 hours Medium impact

Permission: Check your lease before making any change. When in doubt, ask your landlord in writing.

What you need

  • Adhesive-backed foam or V-strip weatherstripping
  • A door sweep sized to your door's width
  • A screwdriver, if your sweep mounts with screws rather than adhesive

Where to get it: ordered online for delivery, or in person at the West Lebanon stores, reachable on the fare-free Advance Transit bus. Where to get it has the addresses and the routes.

Steps

  1. Send your landlord a quick written message naming the two items and asking to install them.
  2. Close the door and find where light or a draft comes through the frame.
  3. Clean the frame where the weatherstripping will stick.
  4. Press the strip along the top and side gaps, trimming it to length.
  5. Attach the door sweep along the bottom edge, screws or adhesive depending on the kit.
  6. Open and close the door once to check it still latches properly.
What if my landlord says no

Ask whether they would rather do it themselves, since it is a cheap, quick fix that benefits the unit either way. A rolled towel at the base of the door is a fully reversible stopgap that needs no permission and no adhesive at all.

Why this works

An exterior door is framed with small manufacturing and settling gaps around its edges, and the bottom gap in particular is often big enough to see daylight through. Weatherstripping and a sweep close that gap so conditioned air stays inside instead of leaking out around the door.

Savings

Estimates are typical ranges, not guarantees. What you actually save depends on your building, your heating system, and the weather.
