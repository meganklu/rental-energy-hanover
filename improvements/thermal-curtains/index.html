Hang thermal curtains

A lined curtain closed at dusk puts a still layer of air between you and a cold window.

Ask your landlord first Mostly comes off at move-out
$25 to $75 1 to 2 hours Medium impact

What you need

  • Lined or thermal curtains wide enough to overlap the window frame on both sides
  • A rod, or a tension rod that sits inside the window recess and needs no screws
  • If the existing rod stays up, nothing else

Where to get it

Before you drill anything

A tension rod inside the window recess holds by friction and leaves no holes, so it needs no permission. A bracket screwed into the wall or the frame does leave holes, and that is the version to ask about first. Most leases treat holes in a wall as damage rather than decoration, so get a yes in writing before the drill comes out.

Steps

  1. Measure the window opening, then buy curtains wider than it. Overlap at the sides is what stops air sliding around the edge.
  2. Hang the rod as high and as wide as it will go, so the curtain covers frame as well as glass.
  3. Close the curtains at dusk, before the room starts losing heat, rather than at bedtime.
  4. Open them again on any window that gets direct sun during the day.
  5. Keep the hem clear of a radiator or a baseboard heater underneath the window. A curtain lying across a heat source traps the heat behind it.

Why this works

A window is the thinnest part of a wall. Glass conducts heat out of a room far faster than the wall around it, and on a cold night the air touching the inside of the pane cools, drops to the floor and pulls warmer air in behind it. That slow circulation is why a room can feel drafty with every gap sealed.

A heavy curtain hung close to the wall interrupts it. The still air trapped between curtain and glass is the actual insulator, which is why coverage at the top and sides matters more than the thickness of the fabric. A curtain that leaves a gap at the top lets the loop keep running.

Worth knowing

Estimates are typical ranges, not guarantees. What you actually save depends on your building, your heating system, and the weather.

Sustainable Hanover's weatherization guidance makes the point that sealing and insulating lower energy costs more than replacing windows does, and names cellular shades and storm windows as the window-side measures worth pairing with that work. Curtains are the version of that a tenant can put up and take down.

If you want the storm-window version, the committee runs a Window Inserts program that builds custom interior inserts held in by friction, with nothing fixed to the frame.
