Shut down before a break

An empty apartment still costs money to heat. Turn it down without turning it off.

Ask your landlord first Comes off at move-out
Free Under 30 minutes Medium impact

Safety

  • Do not turn the heat off. The American Red Cross says to leave the heat on when you go away in cold weather, set no lower than 55°F.
  • A pipe that freezes can burst, and the water damage lands on you, your roommates and everything in the apartment.
  • Find the water shut-off valve now, while nothing is wrong, so you can tell a roommate or a neighbor where it is.
  • Never thaw a frozen pipe with a torch. The EPA says to thaw slowly with warm air from a hair dryer.
  • Tell your landlord you are away and for how long, and ask what they want the heat set to. In a building where they pay for heat, that setting is their call.

What you need

  • Nothing to buy. Twenty minutes and a look around before you lock the door.

Steps

  1. Set the heat back rather than off. Somewhere between your normal setting and the 55°F floor, unless your landlord asks for something specific.
  2. If it is going to be very cold while you are away, the Red Cross advises holding one temperature day and night rather than running a night setback.
  3. Open the cabinet doors under the kitchen and bathroom sinks, so warm air can reach the pipes behind them. Move cleaners and chemicals out of reach first.
  4. Unplug what does not need to stay on. Chargers, monitors, a TV, a games console, a printer, a microwave with a clock. A switched power strip does the lot in one go.
  5. Leave the fridge running with the door shut, or empty it, defrost it and prop the door open. Half-measures grow mould.
  6. Close every window and lock it. A window latched but not locked can be pulled open by wind.
  7. Close the curtains. There is nobody there to want the daylight, and a covered window loses less heat overnight.
  8. Take the bins out and run the taps for a moment before you go.
  9. Agree with your roommates who is last out, and who is checking on the place if anyone stays in town.

Why this works

How much heat a building loses depends on how far the inside temperature sits above the outside one. Over a three-week break in a Hanover winter, that gap is what you are paying for, every hour, in a set of rooms nobody is in.

Narrowing the gap is the whole saving, and it is why the setting matters more than the number of days. What stops you closing the gap completely is plumbing rather than comfort: below a point the water in the pipes starts to freeze, and water expands as it freezes until something gives.

Savings

Estimates are typical ranges, not guarantees. What you actually save depends on your building, your heating system, and the weather.

The U.S. Department of Energy estimates that using a programmable thermostat to set the heat back while you are away or asleep can save about 10% a year on heating and cooling costs. A break is the longest away period in the year, so it is the one where a setback has the most hours to work with.

If heat is included in your rent, the setback saves your landlord money rather than you. The unplugging still counts, because electricity is usually the bill with your own name on it.
