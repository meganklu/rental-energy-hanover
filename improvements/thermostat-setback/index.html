Set your thermostat back

Turning the heat down while you are out or asleep typically uses less energy than holding one temperature all day.

No permission needed Comes off at move-out
Free Under 30 minutes High impact

What you need

  • Nothing. Just your existing thermostat.

Steps

  1. Find your normal comfortable temperature and note it.
  2. Before bed or before leaving for the day, lower the setting a few degrees.
  3. If your thermostat is programmable, schedule the setbackTurning your heat down for a stretch of time, such as overnight or while you are out, and letting it come back up later, instead of holding one temperature all day.Read the full entry so you do not have to remember it.
  4. Set it back up when you return or wake, rather than leaving it low all day.

Why this works

A common belief is that reheating a cold room costs more than leaving the heat on the whole time. It does not. A heating system loses more total energy holding one temperature for hours than it does bringing a room back up after a lower stretch, because the amount of heat a home loses to the outside is roughly proportional to how far the indoor temperature sits above the outdoor one. A lower setting for part of the day narrows that gap and saves energy overall.

Savings

Estimates are typical ranges, not guarantees. What you actually save depends on your building, your heating system, and the weather.

The U.S. Department of Energy estimates that using a programmable thermostat to set the heat back while you are away or asleep can save about 10% a year on heating and cooling costs.
