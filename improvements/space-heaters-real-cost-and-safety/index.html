Use a space heater safely

A space heater runs on electricity and typically costs more to run than it seems. Safety comes first.

No permission needed Comes off at move-out
$25 to $75 Under 30 minutes Medium impact

Safety

  • Keep at least 3 feet of clearance between the heater and anything flammable: bedding, curtains, furniture, papers.
  • Never leave a space heater running unattended, including while you sleep.
  • Plug it directly into a wall outlet. Never use a power strip or extension cord.
  • Buy only a heater listed by a recognized testing laboratory.
  • Use an electric space heater only. A fuel-burning heater (kerosene, propane) is a carbon monoxide risk indoors and is not something this guide covers as an option.

Heating equipment is a leading cause of home fires in the United States. These steps are what actually prevents that.

What you need

  • A listed electric space heater

Check the box for a recognized testing-laboratory listing before you buy.

Where to get it

Steps

  1. Pick a spot with 3 feet of clear space on every side, on a hard, level floor.
  2. Plug it straight into a wall outlet, not a shared power strip.
  3. Turn it off and unplug it when you leave the room or go to sleep.
  4. Check your bill after a week of regular use so you know what it is actually costing you.

Why this works

An electric space heater turns electricity directly into heat, and every watt it draws shows up on your electric bill the same way a hair dryer or a space heater's own nameplate wattage would suggest. It is easy to underestimate because the heater itself is small. Reading your own bill after using one for a week is the most honest way to see the real cost for your unit, rather than a number that will not match your actual rate.

Savings

Estimates are typical ranges, not guarantees. What you actually save depends on your building, your heating system, and the weather.
