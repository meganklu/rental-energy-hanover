Find your drafts

Renter basics

You cannot seal a gap you have not found. A draft is air moving through a gap that should not be there, and you can find one with nothing but your hand.

How to check

  1. Close the door or window and run a bare hand slowly along the edge and the bottom.
  2. Feel for a cold line or a faint moving breeze, not just a cold room in general.
  3. Check every exterior door, every window, and outlet and switch plates on outside walls.
  4. In the basement, check where the foundation meets the wood framing above it, and around the attic hatch if you can safely reach it.

True or false: if you cannot feel a breeze, there is no draft.

False. A draft can just make a spot feel cold without an obvious breeze, especially right at a window or door edge. Feel for a temperature change, not only for moving air.

Borrow a thermal camera

Everything above finds a draft you can feel. A thermal camera finds the ones you cannot, because it photographs surface temperature rather than air movement: a cold stripe along a wall, a dark square where the attic hatch is, the outline of a stud bay with nothing in it.

Howe Library lends one, free, through its Library of Things, along with an electricity usage monitor. Loan periods for those items are not listed on their page, so ask at the desk. It turns this page from a walkthrough into a measurement, and a photograph is a far better thing to attach to an email to your landlord than a description of a draught.

What to do next

Windows and the exterior door are the two most common places students find a draft in a Hanover rental.

  • Seal your windows with film
  • Seal your door with a sweep
