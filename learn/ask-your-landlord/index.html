Ask your landlord

A specific written request with a program attached gets further than a complaint about the cold.

Renter basics

Why the ask usually fails

Most of the things that would make the biggest difference to your bill are not yours to do. Insulation, windows and the heating system belong to whoever owns the building, and the money they would save shows up on a bill they do not pay. About has the long version of that problem.

What that means in practice is that "the apartment is cold" is not a request. It names a feeling, asks for nothing in particular, and gives the person reading it no way to say yes. A request that works looks different.

What a good ask has in it

  1. One specific thing. Not "can something be done about the heat", but "can a door sweep be fitted to the back door". One item, one decision.
  2. Evidence you actually looked. Where the draft is, which window, what you already tried. Find your drafts is how to get that, and the Howe Library lends a thermal camera that photographs it.
  3. What it costs them. Small and cheap gets said yes to. Name the figure if you know it.
  4. Who pays. If a program covers it, name the program and link it. If you are offering to buy or fit it yourself, say so plainly.
  5. What is in it for them. A unit that holds heat is cheaper to run between tenants and easier to let. If they pay for the heat, the saving is theirs.
  6. A date. "Before the end of October" gives the request somewhere to go. An open-ended ask sits open-ended.

Put it in writing

Email rather than a conversation in the driveway. Not because anyone expects a fight, but because a written request has a date on it, says exactly what was asked, and can be referred back to without anybody having to remember. If you do end up talking in person, send a short email afterwards saying what you agreed.

Keep it short. A landlord reading five lines answers them. A landlord reading five paragraphs answers later.

Programs worth naming

The strongest version of this ask is the one where you are not asking them to spend money. Some of these are things they apply for, so what you are really sending is a link and a reason to click it. Programs has the detail.

  • NHSaves weatherization. Rebates and low-rate financing for insulation and air sealing. Their project, their application.
  • The income-eligible program. Covers the full weatherization cost for households that qualify, and renters are eligible. It needs the landlord's approval, so this is one where the ask is the whole thing.
  • Sustainable Hanover's Window Inserts. Custom interior storm windows held in by friction, with nothing screwed or glued to the frame. The committee's own page notes that some landlords buy them for their tenants, particularly where the landlord is paying for the heat.

If the answer is no

Most of this site is the things you can do without asking anybody. Start there: sealing, curtains, setback, and how the heat gets used are all yours, and together they are not a small share of the bill.

A no on an improvement is different from a home that is not warm enough to live in. New Hampshire sets a minimum standard for heat that a lease cannot sign away, and Your rights covers what that is and who to call.
