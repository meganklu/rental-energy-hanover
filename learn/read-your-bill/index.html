Read your bill

Renter basics

Two numbers on your electric bill explain almost everything: how much you used, and what each unit of that costs. Multiply them and you have your bill.

Where to find each number

  • Usage in kWh, usually near the middle of the bill, sometimes with a chart of the last several months.
  • The rate, in cents or dollars per kWh, often printed near the usage figure or in a rate schedule.
  • The total, at the bottom, which is usage times rate plus any fixed monthly charges.

Quick check

True or false: the total dollar amount on your bill tells you how much electricity you used.

The answer

False

The dollar total mixes your usage with the rate and any fixed charges. The kWh figure is the actual amount of energy you used, and it is the number that changes when you change a habit.

What a kilowatt hour actually is

A kilowatt hourThe unit your electric bill is priced in.Read the full entry is one unit of energy: a thousand watts drawn for one hour. A 100-watt device left on for ten hours uses one. Anything that makes heat is in a different class entirely, and its nameplate will tell you so, which is the whole reason a space heater costs what it does to run.

Your bill is that number multiplied by a rate, plus fixed charges that do not move whatever you do. Which is why the useful question is never "why is the bill high" but "which line moved, and what did I plug in". Howe Library lends an electricity usage monitor if you want to measure a particular device rather than guess at it.

Who serves Hanover

Liberty Utilities (Granite State Electric) is the electric utility serving Hanover. Knowing your provider is what lets you find your actual rate instead of guessing at one.
