Accessibility statement

What this site is built to meet, what has actually been checked, and what has not been tested yet.

What we are building to

We build this site to meet WCAG 2.2 Level AA. That is a requirement while each part is written, not a goal for later.

It has not yet been fully tested against that standard, and we have not yet tested it with screen reader users. So this page states a commitment and a status. It does not claim conformance, because a claim would need an audit that has not happened.

What is built in

  • Every control works from the keyboard alone, in a visible order, with a focus outline that is never removed.
  • Nothing depends on color to make sense. Every status carries text, and usually an icon as well.
  • The doll house on the home page is a drawing, and everything in it is also a plain list of links under it. That list is present at every screen width and is the route a screen reader, a keyboard, or a phone-sized window takes.
  • Every page works with JavaScript switched off. Scripts filter, sort and remember. They do not carry the content.
  • Body text is never smaller than 16px, and the layout reflows to a 320px-wide window with no sideways scrolling, which is also what a desktop browser does at 400% zoom.
  • Text sits on its background at a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1, and most of it is well above that. The measurements are recorded in the design documentation.

Motion

This site moves more than most. Diagrams loop so you can watch a mechanism you cannot otherwise see, and a few things shift as you scroll.

  • If your device is set to reduce motion, everything here holds still from the first frame. You do not have to ask twice.
  • A Reduce motion switch sits in the bottom-left corner of every page, for a borrowed laptop or a setting you would rather not change system-wide. It works with JavaScript off on the page you are on.
  • A Pause button appears next to it on any page with something looping.
  • You can also force motion back on if your device asks for reduced motion and you would rather watch a diagram move.
  • Nothing on this site starts moving on load, and no carousel advances on its own.

Freeze any animation here at any point and the screen still makes sense. Motion never carries information that is not also written down.

What has been checked

  • Automated scans with axe DevTools and Lighthouse, before each change goes live.
  • A keyboard-only pass on each component as it was built.
  • Contrast measured on every design token, and re-measured whenever one changes.
  • Both reduced-motion paths, the device setting and the switch on the page.
  • A spot check at 320px wide and at 200% zoom.

Automated tools catch roughly a third of accessibility problems. A clean scan is a signal that the obvious things are right. It is not evidence that the site conforms.

What has not been tested

These are known gaps, listed so that nobody has to discover them the hard way.

  • No screen reader pass has been run on any page. Semantic HTML and native elements are doing that work so far, which is the reason we expect it to hold, not evidence that it does.
  • The doll house in particular has not been screen reader tested. This is the largest open risk on the site. The plain link list under it exists so that the content is reachable regardless.
  • The site has not been tested with a disabled user.
  • No criterion-by-criterion audit against WCAG 2.2 AA has been done.
  • Narrow widths reflow and are readable. Nothing is tuned for touch, and phone layouts are the first item in the next version.

Tell us about a barrier

If something here does not work for you, we want to hear about it, including the things listed above. Email sustainablehanovernh@gmail.com and say which page it was and what happened. A report about a page in the untested list above is as useful as one about anything else.

If you need something on this site in another format, ask at the same address.
