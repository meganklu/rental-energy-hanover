What to check before you sign

A viewing lasts about ten minutes and usually happens in warm weather. Here is what to look at, and the two questions that decide what winter will cost you.

Renter basics

Ask these two things first

Everything else on this page is worth less than the answers to these. Ask them before the viewing if you can, because they decide whether the apartment is affordable rather than whether it is nice.

A landlord who does not know, or will not say, has told you something too. Get the answer in writing either way, because "heat included" in conversation and "heat included" in a lease are different things.

  1. What heats it, and who pays for that? Fuel oil, propane, natural gas, electric baseboard and a heat pump all cost different amounts to run, and whether the bill comes to you or is covered by rent changes the math completely. How to tell what you are looking at.
  2. What did it actually cost last winter? Ask for the real figure for the coldest months rather than an average across the year. Hanover's heating season runs October through April and peaks December through February, so a monthly average hides the months that hurt.

Take this to the viewing

Ten minutes, and most of this is looking rather than asking. A cold apartment in August looks exactly like a warm one, so you are looking for the things that give it away.

  • Find the heat source in every room you will use. A bedroom with no radiator, no baseboard and no vent will be the cold room. Check behind furniture, and check whether one is already blocked by where the furniture has to go.
  • Windows. Single pane or double? Look at the edge of the glass in the corner: two panes show a visible gap between them. Do the sashes lock shut, and do they rattle when you push?
  • Doors to outside. Kneel down and look for daylight under the door and along the frame. Daylight means air.
  • The basement. Look at the top of the basement wall where it meets the floor above. Bare framing there is where a lot of a house's heat leaves.
  • The thermostat. Is there one, is it in your unit, and does it control your rooms or the whole building? A thermostat you cannot reach is a bill you cannot change.
  • Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms. Present, on every level, and outside the sleeping areas. This one is not about money.
  • Photograph everything. Every room, every existing mark on a wall, every appliance. This is the record that protects your deposit later, and it takes two minutes now.

Read for these in the lease

  • Which utilities are included, named one by one. "Utilities included" can mean water only. Heat, electricity and hot water are three separate answers.
  • What you are allowed to change. Look for language about adhesives, screws, painting and fixtures. This is what decides whether a door sweep is a conversation or a problem.
  • How the security deposit is handled, and what counts as normal wear.
  • How to report a repair, and to whom. Knowing this before you need it is worth more than it sounds.
  • The dates. A nine-month lease changes which improvements pay for themselves at all.

Legal: This site provides general information, not legal advice. Leases and situations differ. New Hampshire does set a floor on heat regardless of what a lease says, and Your rights covers that.

Once you have signed

Take the photographs again on the day you move in, dated. Then start with reading your first bill and finding your drafts, which are the two things that make everything else on this site apply to your actual apartment.
