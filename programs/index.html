Programs

Most energy programs are written for whoever owns the building. Here is which ones reach a renter, and what your landlord has to sign for the rest.

Why so many of these need your landlord

WeatherizingKeeping indoor air from escaping and outdoor air from getting in, which is how you cut what heating costs.Read the full entry an apartment means changing the building, and the building is not yours. That is the whole reason a program asks for an owner's signature: it is a permanent improvement to someone else's property.

It is also why the improvement often does not happen. The landlord pays for insulation and you get the lower heating bill, so neither of you has a reason to move first. That gap has a name, the split incentiveWhat happens when the person who would pay for an upgrade, your landlord, is not the person who benefits from the lower bill it produces, you.Read the full entry, and About lays it out. The practical answer is usually to bring your landlord a program that pays for the work rather than a request that they pay for it.

    Income-Eligible Energy Assistance Program, through NHSaves

    If your household qualifies on income, this covers the full cost of weatherizing the unit. Not a discount and not a rebate you claim back later. Your local Community Action Agency determines eligibility and runs the work.

    Show more Show less
    Who runs it
    NHSaves, the utility coalition, through Community Action Agencies
    Eligibility
    Based on household income. The Community Action Agency decides, not the utility and not your landlord
    What your landlord signs
    Approval for the work, since it changes the building

  • Check the current income limits

    Fuel and electric assistance, through the NH Department of Energy

    Help with the bill itself rather than with the building. Eligibility runs off a share of New Hampshire's state median income, and the limits are set for each year and each household size, so read them fresh rather than from memory.

    Show more Show less
    Who runs it
    New Hampshire Department of Energy, through Community Action Agencies
    Eligibility
    An income threshold that scales with household size, updated each year
    What your landlord signs
    Nothing, if the bill is in your name

  • Your landlord applies, not you

    Home Energy Performance, through NHSaves

    The general weatherization path for a building whose owner does not qualify for the income-eligible program above. It starts with an energy audit and can be financed with an unsecured loan at a reduced rate, which depends on the utility serving the building. Funding is time-limited, so the terms are worth checking before anyone counts on them.

    Show more Show less
    Who runs it
    NHSaves, the coalition of Eversource, Liberty, New Hampshire Electric Co-op and Unitil
    What it is useful for as a renter
    Something concrete to send a landlord who says the work is unaffordable

  • Renters can use this

    Window Inserts, through Sustainable Hanover

    Custom interior storm windows, built to the measurements of your own windows at a community build. A pine frame wrapped in clear film traps an insulating layer of air in front of the glass, and foam weatherstripping around the edge seals it to the frame. It is held in by friction alone.

    Show more Show less

    That last detail is what makes this the one committee program aimed squarely at a rental. Nothing is screwed, glued or nailed to the window, so nothing has to be put right at move-out, and the inserts pull out in seconds if you need the window open in a hurry. Sustainable Hanover's own page notes that some landlords buy inserts for their tenants, which is worth knowing if yours is the one paying for the heat.

    Who runs it
    Sustainable Hanover, with the Norwich and Lyme energy committees, using the WindowDressers approach
    Eligibility
    Hanover residents and employees living within 30 minutes of Hanover. You sign up, then trained volunteers come and measure
    Cost
    Custom priced per window, since every insert is built to a specific opening. Ask for a quote rather than budgeting from a figure
    Subsidies
    Priority and subsidies go to low-income households qualifying for fuel assistance or using Listen, The Haven, COVER or the Town Welfare Office
    When
    Builds run in rounds rather than year-round. The 2026 Community Build is 1 to 7 October at the Church of Christ at Dartmouth College. Check the page for the current round before counting on a date
    What your landlord signs
    Nothing, since nothing is fixed to the building. Tell them anyway

    The Library of Things, at Howe Library

    Not an energy program, and the most immediately useful thing on this page. Howe Library lends a thermal camera, which shows you where the heat is leaving as a picture rather than as a guess; an LED lighting sample kit, for seeing what a bulb actually looks like before buying six of them; and an electricity usage monitor, which tells you what a given device really draws.

    Show more Show less

    Free, needs nobody's permission, and it turns two pages on this site from advice into measurement. Find your drafts is much easier with the camera, and cutting phantom load stops being guesswork with the monitor.

    Who runs it
    Howe Library, Hanover
    Eligibility
    Library borrowing. Loan periods for these items are not listed, so ask at the desk or call 603-643-4120
    What your landlord signs
    Nothing

    Library of Things

  • Renters can use this Only if the electric bill is in your name

    Hanover Community Power

    Hanover buys electricity supply on behalf of the town rather than leaving everyone on the utility's default. If the electric account is in your name, you are the customer, and the choice of supply tier is yours rather than your landlord's. Liberty still delivers the power, handles outages and sends the bill.

    Show more Show less

    Tiers differ in price and in how much renewable content they carry, and rates change roughly every six months, so read the current ones rather than any figure quoted second-hand. Opting up, opting down and opting out are all possible. New Hampshire Electric Co-op customers are not enrolled automatically and have to opt in.

    Who runs it
    The Town of Hanover, through the Community Power Coalition of New Hampshire
    What your landlord signs
    Nothing, if the bill is in your name. If it is in theirs, this is their choice and not yours

    Hanover Community Power

  • Your landlord applies, not you

    Weatherize, through Sustainable Hanover

    The committee's guidance for whoever owns the building: insulating and air sealing, mostly in the basement and the attic, done by a certified contractor after an energy audit. Sustainable Hanover makes the point that this lowers energy costs more than replacing windows does, which is a useful thing to have in writing when an owner proposes new windows instead.

    Show more Show less
    Who runs it
    Sustainable Hanover, pointing at NHSaves-approved and BPI-certified contractors
    What it is useful for as a renter
    The page to send a landlord who asks what weatherizing actually involves

Programs that are not for you

These come up in a search and cost an afternoon to rule out. Ruling them out here is faster.

  • Homeowners only

    COVER Home Repair

    An Upper Valley nonprofit that does winter weatherization, draft sealing and urgent repairs. Hanover is inside the area it serves, and the program requires the homeowner to participate. There is no path where a landlord initiates it on a tenant's behalf. Worth knowing only as something you could point an owner-occupant landlord toward.

How to ask your landlord

Ask in writing, name the specific program, and say what it costs them. "Would you consider insulating" is a request. "NHSaves has a program that covers the cost of weatherizing this unit, and the application starts with an energy audit" is an offer, and it is a much easier thing to say yes to.

Keep the thread. A written request is also the record you want later if the apartment turns out not to meet the minimum heat standard.
