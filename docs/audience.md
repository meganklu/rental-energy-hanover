## 1. Primary audience

Dartmouth undergraduate students, ages 19–23, living off-campus in Hanover, typically in 9–12 month leases, usually sharing a unit with 2–5 roommates, most of whom have never paid a utility bill.

**Rough size:** Approximately 600–700 Dartmouth students live off campus, based on Dartmouth admissions information. This includes college-owned off-campus apartments.

## 4. Constraints that make student renters different

<!-- GUIDANCE: These are the constraints that make generic "home weatherization" advice
     wrong for this audience. Get this right and the site writes itself. -->

- Can't make permanent changes — limited to reversible improvements or requests to the landlord.
- Short tenancy — payback periods matter when a lease may only last a semester or year.
- Split incentives with the landlord — students may pay the energy bill while landlords control major improvements.
- Shared decision-making with roommates — heating preferences and thermostat settings affect everyone.
- Low or no budget — improvements need to be free or very low cost whenever possible.
- Doesn't know who to ask — may be unsure whether to contact the landlord, property manager, utility, or town.
- Leaves for winter break — an empty apartment in a New Hampshire winter creates different heating and energy concerns.
- Limited experience with home maintenance — managing heating and energy bills may be new to students.
- Too busy — school, work, and other responsibilities can make energy efficiency a low priority.
- Don't expect to stay long enough to see long-term improvements — students may be less motivated to invest in changes that benefit future tenants.

## 5. Personas

### Persona A — The Don't Know Renter

| | |
|---|---|
| **Situation** | Undergraduate living off-campus with 2–4 roommates on a 9–12 month lease. Pays attention to monthly expenses and notices when the apartment is cold or the utility bill is high, but has little experience with home maintenance or energy efficiency. Often does not have a car, making access to supplies or services more difficult. |
| **Goal** | Reduce energy costs and make the apartment more comfortable without violating the lease or creating problems with the landlord. |
| **Trigger for visiting** | Notices a high utility bill, feels a draft around a window or door, or realizes the apartment is difficult to keep warm. |
| **Blockers** | Doesn't know what improvements are possible, where to get materials, or whether permission is required. Nervous about upsetting the landlord or being blamed for damage. Limited transportation and limited money can also make improvements harder. |
| **What success looks like for them** | Finds a simple, affordable improvement they can confidently make or a specific request they can make to their landlord. |
| **Quote** | "I want to make my apartment more energy efficient, but I don't really know what I'm allowed to do." |

### Persona B — The Don't Care Renter

| | |
|---|---|
| **Situation** | Undergraduate living off-campus with roommates on a short-term lease. Has heard about energy efficiency and weatherization but is not particularly concerned about utility costs or the apartment's efficiency. Has a busy schedule and expects to move within a year or two. |
| **Goal** | Stay comfortable and avoid spending unnecessary time or effort dealing with the apartment. |
| **Trigger for visiting** | A particularly high utility bill, an uncomfortable apartment, a roommate suggesting an improvement, or being directed to the site through a campus or community resource. |
| **Blockers** | Too busy, not motivated by long-term savings, and unlikely to invest time or money in improvements that will primarily benefit future tenants or the landlord. |
| **What success looks like for them** | Quickly finds one or two easy actions that require little time, money, or coordination and can immediately improve comfort or reduce energy use. |
| **Quote** | "I know there are probably things I could do, but I'm only here for a year and I have enough other things to worry about." |

## 7. Context of use

- **Device:** Computer, likely while actively researching options for their apartment.
- **Session length:** A short research session, similar to a shopping trip where the user compares options before deciding what to do.
- **Emotional state:** Curious, concerned about cost or comfort, or looking for a practical solution to a specific problem.
- **Implication for design:** Make options easy to compare. Clearly show cost, effort, permission requirements, expected benefits, and whether an improvement is reversible. Users should be able to quickly determine which options apply to their situation.

## 8. Anti-personas

<!-- GUIDANCE: Who we are explicitly not designing for. Prevents feature creep. -->

- Homeowners looking to improve their homes.
- Renters looking for major or expensive retrofits.
- Users interested in projects requiring major electrical, structural, or heating-system changes.
- HVAC professionals, contractors, or other users looking for technical installation guidance.
