## 2. Round 1 — Discovery (before building)


- **Method:** Short semi-structured interviews
- **Participants:** 2 Dartmouth students who live in rental housing. Participants were students the project makers knew lived in a rental but were not closely acquainted with.
- **When:** August 2026
- **Where:** Hanover, NH
- **Duration:** Approximately 10 minutes
- **Incentive:** None


### Interview guide


1. "Tell me about your current or most recent apartment. What is the heating like? Is there anything you would change about it?"
2. "What do you know about improving the energy efficiency of a rental apartment?"
3. "Have you ever made any changes to your apartment to make it warmer, cooler, or more energy efficient?"
4. "Have you ever looked into weatherization or other ways to reduce your energy use?"
5. "If you wanted to make your apartment more energy efficient, what would you do first?"
6. "What, if anything, has stopped you from making changes?"
7. "Have you ever been unsure whether you were allowed to make a change to your apartment?"
8. "Have you ever wanted to ask your landlord to make an improvement? What happened?"
9. "What kinds of information or resources would have made this easier?"
10. "What would make you more likely to actually make a change?"


### What we'll do with the results


Use the interview findings to create personas representing different types of student renters and the challenges they face when considering energy-efficiency improvements. These personas will guide the project and help us tailor the website to the specific problems identified by students.


## 3. Round 2 — Usability testing (on the website)


- **Method:** Scenario-based testing using the completed website, followed by a short interview.
- **Participants:** Dartmouth student renters
- **When:** If the project continues,
- **What we'll test:** Whether students can use the website to identify practical solutions to realistic heating and energy-efficiency problems and whether they find the information helpful.


### Tasks


| # | Scenario | Success looks like | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | "Your apartment is cold and you want to find something you can do without making a permanent change." | Student finds at least one appropriate reversible improvement. | |
| T2 | "You are paying more than expected for energy and want to find a way to reduce your costs." | Student finds an option that could reasonably address their situation. | |
| T3 | "You want your landlord to make an improvement, but you are not sure what to ask for." | Student finds a specific recommendation and understands what they can ask their landlord to do. | |


### Post-test interview


After students complete the scenarios, ask:


1. "Did the website help you find a good solution?"
2. "What solution did you come up with?"
3. "Do you think you would be able to implement this solution?"
4. "Do you think the cost and timing are realistic for you?"


### What we'll record


The solutions students identify, whether they believe they could implement those solutions, whether the cost and timing seem realistic, points of confusion, and comments about the usefulness of the website.


## 4. Ethics and consent


- **Consent:** Participants were informed of the purpose of the interviews and that participation was voluntary.
- **Recording:** Notes only
- **Anonymity:** Participants are kept anonymous. No names or identifying information will be included in the research findings or final project.
- **Data handling:** Data held on google drive folders.
