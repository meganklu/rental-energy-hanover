# Glossary

> **Status:** ● Complete · **Last updated:** 2026-08-19 · **Owner:** Megan

This file has two jobs: it's the page a first-time renter lands on when a term confuses them, and
it's the internal check that keeps the site using one word for one thing. Every entry defines the
term in a few sentences a 19-year-old would understand, then says why it matters in money or comfort
terms. Anything with a number cites [sources.md](sources.md).

## Conventions

- **Preferred term** is what we use site-wide. Alternatives are listed so we don't drift.
- Link the first use of any term on a page to its glossary entry, per
  [accessibility.md](accessibility.md) §4.
- If a definition needs jargon to explain it, the definition isn't done.
- Terms are alphabetized. Add new ones in place rather than at the end.

## Terms

### Baseboard electric heat

**Also called:** Electric baseboard, resistance heat
**Definition:** A heating element inside a long, low unit that runs along the bottom of a wall,
warming the room directly instead of blowing air through ducts or circulating hot water.
**Why you care:** It's almost always billed straight to your own electric account, unlike heat
that's included in rent, so how much you run it changes your bill directly. Electric heat —
commonly baseboard — is the second most common heating type in Hanover rentals, after fuel oil.
**Source:** S12d

### Building envelope

**Also called:** Envelope, building shell
**Definition:** Everything that separates the inside of your home from the outside: exterior
walls, roof, windows, doors, and foundation. Think of it as the shell around your heated space.
**Why you care:** Most weatherization advice — sealing, insulating, weatherstripping — is about
closing gaps in the building envelope. Knowing the term helps the rest of the site make sense, but
you'll rarely need to say it yourself: this site points at the specific window, door, or wall
instead.

### Draft

**Also called:** Air leak
**Definition:** Air moving through a gap that shouldn't be there — cold air leaking in around a
window or door, or warm air leaking out. You feel it as a cold spot or a faint breeze even with
the windows closed.
**Why you care:** Finding your drafts is the first step before anything else on this site — you
can't seal a gap you haven't found.

### Forced air / radiator / heat pump

**Definition:** Three ways heat gets delivered to a room, besides baseboard. Forced air pushes
heated air through ducts and vents — you'll feel it blowing. A radiator (steam or hot water) heats
a metal unit that warms the room by radiating heat, with no fan. A heat pump moves heat rather than
generating it, and can usually cool the room in summer too.
**Why you care:** Your heat type decides which improvements on this site apply to you. For example, sealing a
duct only matters if you have forced air, and a radiator or baseboard should never be blocked or
covered no matter which one you have.

### Habitability / minimum heat standard

**Also called:** Warranty of habitability
**Definition:** The legal minimum condition a landlord must maintain for a home to be rentable at
all. In New Hampshire that includes heat: your landlord must be able to keep every habitable room
at an average of at least 65°F.
**Why you care:** If your apartment can't hit that standard there's a specific process for reporting it.
**Source:** S7, S6a

### kWh

**Also called:** Kilowatt-hour
**Definition:** The unit your electric bill is priced in. It measures how much energy you used
over time, not how much power something draws in a single moment.
**Why you care:** It's the number on your bill, and multiplying it by your rate (cents per kWh) is
how you see what something actually costs you.

### Payback period

**Definition:** How long it takes for what you save on energy to add up to what you spent on the
upgrade.
**Why you care:** On a 9-month lease, a $75 upgrade with a 3-year payback will not benefit you directly. This site favors low-cost, fast-payback improvements for exactly that reason.

### Phantom load

**Also called:** Vampire draw, standby power
**Definition:** The electricity a device uses while it's plugged in but turned off or in standby. This includes chargers, game consoles, TVs, and anything with an always-on clock or light.
**Why you care:** It adds up quietly on a bill you're already paying, and it's one of the few
things you can cut at zero cost and with no landlord permission.
**Source:** S9

### R-value

**Also called:** Thermal resistance
**Definition:** A measure of how well a material resists heat flow. The higher the number, the
better it insulates. It's printed on insulation, window film, and some weatherstripping packaging.
**Why you care:** When you're comparing two window insulation kits, a higher R-value means it'll
do more to keep the cold out.

### Security deposit

**Also called:** Damage deposit
**Definition:** Money you pay your landlord up front, refundable at move-out minus the cost of
damage beyond normal wear and tear.
**Why you care:** Any change you make that isn't easily reversible risks part of that deposit.
Every improvement on this site is flagged as reversible or not.

### Split incentive

**Also called:** Split-incentive problem
**Definition:** What happens when the person who'd pay for an upgrade (your landlord) isn't the
person who benefits from the lower bill it produces (you), so neither has a full reason to act on
it alone.
**Why you care:** This problem is why a lot of good weatherization advice doesn't happen in rentals by
default, and it's why this site separates what you can do yourself from what you have to ask your
landlord for.
**Source:** S8a — cited for the split-incentive framing only, not as an available program (it
describes a Massachusetts program, not one Hanover renters can enroll in).

### Thermostat setback

**Also called:** Night setback, temperature setback
**Definition:** Turning your heat down for a stretch of time, such as overnight or while you're out,
and letting it come back up later, instead of holding one temperature all day.
**Why you care:** It's one of the easiest no-cost changes on this site, and it needs no landlord permission.
**Common misconception:** "It costs more to reheat a cold room than to leave the heat on steady."
The opposite is generally true: energy is wasted holding one temperature the whole time you're
away or asleep. This is the energy consumption a setback avoids.
**Source:** S21

### Utilities included

**Definition:** Heat, electricity, or both are already covered by your monthly rent, with no
separate bill in your name for that utility.
**Why you care:** It changes almost every recommendation on this site. If your landlord pays the
heating bill, sealing a draft mostly saves them money, not you. The improvements are still worth asking for, but it
won't lower your own bill.

### Weatherization

**Also called:** Weatherizing (the action), weatherproofing, air sealing and insulating
**Definition:** The purpose of weatherizing is to prevent indoor air from escaping and outdoor air
from entering your home, which helps save on energy costs. Weatherizing your home involves
physical home improvements, such as adding insulation and air sealing cracks, especially in
basement and attic areas. These changes resolve issues like ice dams, moisture, mold, cold spots
and drafts.
**Why you care:** It's the umbrella term for most of what's on this site, and sealing and
insulating are usually the cheapest ways to cut a heating bill.
**Source:** S1
